\section{Abstract}
We have investigated the structural and dielectric properties of two complementary amorphous oxide systems, Ta-Ge-O and Ta-Si-O. We found that these two chemically similar amorphous systems exhibit substantially different composition-property trends. In particular, the dielectric constant of Ta-Ge-O is strongly enhanced in \ce{Ta2O5}-rich films as previously reported, while only slight enhancement was observed for any Ta-Si-O compositions and the index of refraction does not show any appreciable enhancement in either system. We determined the effective polarizabilities of the mixed oxides by directly inferring the density of the films, avoiding the assumption that atomic polarizations are additive. The results confirm that \ce{Ta2O5}-rich compositions in the Ta-Ge-O system exhibit an ionic polarizability that is strongly enhanced over the value expected using the rule of mixtures, which suggests that even small levels of incorporation of \ce{GeO2} have a substantial effect on the vibrational structure of the oxide. In this work we have explored the limitations of the Clausius-Mossotti relation, showing that calculating the dielectric constant using tabulated atomic polarizabilities and experimentally inferred molecular volumes systematically underestimates the dielectric constant for both systems. Only by directly measuring densities and dielectric properties are we able to expose trends that can be definitively interpreted.

\section{Introduction} 
Tantalum oxide and its doped variants have found their place in a variety of applications, owing to their excellent electro-optical properties \cite{Chaneliere1998}. They have been used in optical coatings \cite{Harry2002}, flash-memories/logic gates \cite{Kingon2000}, and more recently are prime candidates for use in novel non-volatile memory devices \cite{Lee2011a}. However, many of the fundamental mechanisms that give rise to these amazing properties of tantalum oxide based systems are still unclear, especially in more complex multicomponent amorphous oxide systems. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial to the rational use of these materials in next generation devices.

We previously reported that mixed amorphous tantalum germanium oxide exhibits a dielectric constant, $\varepsilon_{r}$ as much as 40\% higher than that of pure tantalum oxide \cite{Naoi2012}. Dielectric enhancement has been reported in multicomponent \textit{crystalline} oxides, where enhancement is commonly attributed to a change in crystal structure. For example, substitution of \ce{TiO2} in \ce{Ta2O5} stabilizes the H$^{\prime}$ monoclinic phase resulting in a near fourfold increase in the dielectric constant \cite{Cava1995}, while incorporation of \ce{Al2O3} in \ce{HfO2} stabilizes the (002)-oriented tetragonal phase, which has a dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{r} =47$ \cite{Park2006}.

Enhancement in amorphous oxides has also been reported, but the effects in these systems tend to be less dramatic. Two chemical systems are particularly noteworthy, namely Hf-Si-O and Zr-Si-O. In these and other amorphous dielectrics, the dielectric constant has been found to modestly exceed the values calculated by simple linear interpolation  between the endmember values (rule of mixtures), while it never exceeds the value corresponding to the highest endmember \cite{Wilk2000a}. In these materials the enhancement has been attributed to changes in bond coordination that affect low frequency vibrational modes, although the assertion that the behavior is an intrinsic materials property is controversial \cite{Lucovsky2000, Rignanese2002, Pignedoli2007}.

In this study, we employ the continuous composition spread approach to determine the composition dependence of the dielectric properties of Ta-based amorphous thin film oxides. We focus on two systems: the previously reported $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ system, which showed a dramatic enhancement in $\varepsilon_{r}$ for $x > 0.5$, and  $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$, a more typical system exhibiting little enhancement. Using codeposited composition spreads \cite{VanDover2004a} we are able to measure properties with very fine compositional resolution in materials deposited under identical conditions, allowing robust determination of the composition dependence of film properties. 

We relate the macroscopic dielectric constants to microscopic polarizabilities with no assumptions about the additive nature of polarizabilities associated with individual species. We find that the enhancement in $\varepsilon_{r}$ observed in the Ta-Ge-O system is associated with an unexpected increase in the ionic polarizability, while the electronic polarizability interpolates linearly with composition.  

\section{Experimental Methods}
%Sputtering
Ta-Ge-O and Ta-Si-O composition spreads were prepared by reactive radio frequency (rf) sputtering using 2 inch diameter metal targets in the 90\textdegree \ off-axis configuration \cite{VanDover1998}. Films were sputtered on 75 mm diameter Si wafers precoated with evaporated Cr (30 nm) and Pt (35 nm). Prior to film deposition the chamber was evacuated to a pressure of 10$^{-6}$ Torr. Gas flow rates during deposition were 15 standard cubic centimeters per minute (sccm) Ar and 10 sccm \ce{O2}, with the turbopump throttled to maintain a pressure of 30 mTorr in the chamber. The rf guns were operated at 100 W for Ta, 30 W for Ge, and 20 W for Si. The substrate was also intentionally biased with rf power to achieve a dc potential of -75 V to ensure the formation of a dense film. This substrate bias has been found to preclude formation of columnar voids during deposition \cite{Barron2009}, which is undesirable when characterizing intrinsic film characteristics. The substrate temperature during deposition was below 60 \textcelsius. 

%Ellipsometry
The index of refraction and thickness of the films were determined using a Woollam Variable Angle Spectroscopic Ellipsometer. The ellipsometric parameters $\psi$ and $\delta$ were measured at 65, 70, and 75\textdegree \ incident angles. At each angle the wavelength was varied from 450 - 1000 nm in 100 nm increments, and a simple film/substrate bilayer model was used to fit $\psi$ and $\delta$. The optical constants of the substrate layer were determined prior to film deposition and were fixed during the fitting process. The optical constants of the film were modeled using the Cauchy relation $n(\lambda) =  A + B / \lambda^{2}$. WVASE32 software was used to fit $A$, $B$, and thickness by recursively varying the parameters to minimize the mean squared error. The refractive index was taken to be A, which is the refractive index in the infinite wavelength limit, i.e., well away from absorption edge.

%XRR
The film density was characterized by x-ray reflectivity (XRR) using a Rigaku Smartlab x-ray diffractometer. Standard Cu-K$_{\alpha}$ radiation ($\lambda$ = 1.5418 \AA) was conditioned through a 5.0\textdegree \ soller slit assembly, a 0.05 mm incident slit, and a 2.0 mm height limiting slit. The reflected x-rays were collected by a detector upon passing through a 0.2 mm receiving slit, 5.0\textdegree \ soller slit, and an additional 0.2 mm receiving slit. Measurements were obtained in the $\theta$ - 2$\theta$ geometry by varying the incident angle between 0 and 2\textdegree \ with a step size of 0.067\textdegree. The instrument was manually aligned for each of 21 positions across the wafer. The footprint of the x-ray beam at these glancing angles was approximately 10 mm in the long dimension and 3 mm in the short, adding a finite uncertainty in the measured density in composition space (indicated by error bars). The film density was determined from the critical angle $\theta_{c}$, which was taken to be the angle at which the intensity is half the maximum reflected intensity \cite{Parratt1954}. Given $\theta_{c}$, the mass density was found using,
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\theta_{c}\pi A}{Z r_{e} \lambda N_{A}}
\label{eq1}
\end{equation}
where A is the average atomic mass, $r_{e}$ is the classical electron radius, $\lambda$ is the X-ray wavelength, $N_{A}$ is Avogadro’s number, and Z is the average atomic number \cite{Bahr1993}. Equation \ref{eq1} assumes that $\theta_{c}$ can be related to $\delta$, the scattering term of the x-ray refractive index, through the relation, $\theta_{c} = \sqrt{2\delta}$. We approximate the atomic form factor for the scattering term, $f^{0}_{1}$, by Z, which is obtained by using the composition ratios inferred from electron microprobe measurements described below. 

%FTIR
To gain insight into the vibrational structure of the amorphous films, Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) was performed using a Bruker Hyperion 2000 FT-IR microscope in reflection mode. Measurements were taken in the spectral range of 600 to 2000 cm$^{-1}$. A background signal obtained from the base electrode was subtracted from the raw FTIR spectra to yield the FTIR spectra of the film.  

%LCR measurements
Electrical characterization was performed by fabricating metal-insulator-metal (MIM) structures. After film deposition, Pt counterelectrodes (200 $\mu$m diameter, 350 \AA\ thick) were electron-beam evaporated on to the film using a shadow mask. Contact to the top electrode was made using a BeCu probe, while gold wire was soldered to the bottom electrode using In metal. The complex impedance $\tilde{Z}$ of the MIM capacitors was measured using an HP4284 LCR meter over the range 100 Hz - 100 kHz using 100 mVac excitation. The complex impedance directly yields the capacitance $C=\omega Im(\tilde{Z})$, dissipation factor $\tan\delta = Re(\tilde{Z})/Im(\tilde{Z}) = Re(\tilde{\varepsilon})/Im(\tilde{\varepsilon})$, and dielectric constant $\varepsilon_{r} = d C/S\varepsilon_0$ where $d$ is the thickness of the film and $S$ is the area of the counterelectrode, and $\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space.

%WDS measurements 
Composition analysis was performed with a JEOL 8900 electron microprobe using wavelength-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (WDS). Bulk metal samples containing known compositions of Ta, Ge, and Si were used as calibration standards. Ta-K$_{\alpha}$, Ge-M$_{\alpha}$, and  Si-K$_{\alpha}$ x-rays were diffracted through analytical crystals and collected by a detector. Detector counts were corrected for thin film and absorption effects using JEOL software and compared against the calibration standards to determine relative Ta:Ge and Ta:Si atomic ratios. 

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Dielectric constant and refractive index}
The dielectric constants and dissipation factor of Ta-Ge-O and Ta-Si-O measured at 10 kHz are presented in Fig \ref{fig1}. 
The solid line represents a linear interpolation between endmember values. The dielectric constants of amorphous \ce{Ta2O5} ($\varepsilon_{r} =22$) \cite{Barron2009} and \ce{SiO2} ($\varepsilon_{r}=3.9$) were measured on films prepared using conditions identical to those used for the composition spreads; for \ce{GeO2} we use the reported \cite{Caperaa1989} value $\varepsilon_{r}=6$. The dissipation factor for all compositions is seen to be low ($\tan\delta < 0.05$), indicating good quality films.

For Ta cation fractions $0.4 < x <0.7$, the dielectric constant for both $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ and $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ follows a trend that is consistent with the linear interpolation. For larger values of $x$ the dielectric constant of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ is considerably enhanced above the linear interpolation and reaches a maximum value $\varepsilon_{r} =34$ at $x \approx 0.97$ \cite{Naoi2012}. The dielectric constant of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ shows a  much smaller enhancement at $x \approx 0.9$ (i.e., $\varepsilon_{r} \approx 23$). It is remarkable that in both cases the dielectric constant is found to achieve values higher than that of either endmember oxide, a behavior not seen in other amorphous mixed-oxide systems. We note that a moderate dielectric enhancement in bulk polycrystalline Ta-Si-O was reported by Cava et al. \cite{Cava1996} and that it occurs at roughly the same composition, $x=0.9$. A modest enhancement in dielectric constant over the linearly-interpolated value is also observed for $x<0.4$ in $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$. At $x=0.15$ the dielectric constant is roughly 10, representing an enhancement of $\approx 40$\%. At $x=0.35$ the dielectric constant for $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ is consistent with the linear interpolation, but the slope suggests the possibility of enhancement from smaller Ta cation fractions.

Figure \ref{fig2} shows the refractive index, $n$, of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ and $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$, along with a linear interpolation based on endmember values (measured $n_{\ce{SiO2}}= 1.39$, $n_{\ce{Ta2O5}}= 2.10$ and literature value $n_{\ce{GeO2}}= 1.60$ \cite{Caperaa1989}). 
In contrast to the behavior of $\varepsilon_r$, $n$ shows no enhancement in either the Ta-Si-O or Ta-Ge-O systems. 
Both $\varepsilon_r$ and $n$ measure the electrical response of the system to an electromagnetic field so this discrepancy directly implies that the enhancement is due to a response that operates only at low frequency. The most likely candidate is the introduction/modification of low-frequency phonon modes. It is surprising that the effects of \ce{SiO2} and \ce{GeO2} substitution are so different, considering the chemical and structural similarity of amorphous \ce{GeO2} and \ce{SiO2}. This suggests that simple bond coordination changes (network effects) do not play a dominant role in the enhancement. 

\subsection{Effects of density on the dielectric constant}

The effect of low levels of \ce{Ta2O5} substitution in \ce{SiO2} resembles the anomalous dielectric behavior of amorphous Hf-Si-O and Zr-Si-O \cite{Wilk2000a}. Several conflicting models have been proposed to explain the odd features in those materials. FTIR studies initially suggested that the enhancement was due to fourfold coordinated Zr-O bonds \cite{Lucovsky2000}, although subsequent cluster simulations revealed the significance of higher-coordinated complexes \cite{Kurtz2001}. Calculations by Rignanese et al. related the dielectric constant of Zr-Si-O to structural units \cite{Rignanese2002}. A modest enhancement of $\varepsilon_{r}$ was attributed to ZrO$_{6}$ octahedra, but was not sufficient to account for the large reported enhancement in $\varepsilon_{r}$. Recent large-scale \textit{ab initio} simulations suggest that the inconsistent data for $\varepsilon_{r} (x)$ in amorphous $\textnormal{Zr}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2}$ and $\textnormal{Hf}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2}$ are due to variability in local structure associated with the variety of coordination numbers accommodated by the transition metal \cite{Pignedoli2007}. In that work it was also found that correlation between $\varepsilon_{r}$ and density could not be established, refuting earlier models for dielectric enhancement based on density changes \cite{Kurtz2001,Devine2001}.

\begin{figure}
\noindent\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{chapter_4/fig3}
\centering
\caption{Variation in density as a function of Ta cation fraction for (top) Ta-Si-O and (bottom) Ta-Ge-O, as inferred using X-ray Reflectivity. The solid lines are linear interpolations between the measured densities of the endmembers.}
\label{fig3}
\end{figure}

The anomalies in the composition dependence of the dielectric constant for $x>0.7$ for  $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ and especially $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ are distinctly different than the behavior for $\textnormal{Zr}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2}$ or $\textnormal{Hf}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2}$. This may be due to the fact that for \ce{Ta2O5} polymorphs the variation in $\varepsilon_r$ arises from the modification of the vibrational structure, which does not strongly dependent on bond coordination \cite{Andreoni2010}. Local deviations from stoichiometry, defects, and impurities can also bring about substantial changes in $\varepsilon_{r}$. Our data for $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ also show that density changes may contribute some of the observed effect on $\varepsilon_{r}$. In Figure \ref{fig3}, we plot the density of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ and $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Si}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ as determined by X-ray reflectivity (error bars indicate uncertainty in composition due to the beam dimensions). The densities of the pure endmembers were measured to be 3.7 g/cm$^{3}$ for amorphous \ce{GeO2} and 7.5 g/cm$^{3}$ for amorphous \ce{Ta2O5}, in good agreement with reported values \cite{Weis2000, Four2000}. The density of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ is consistent with the linear interpolation for $0 <x<0.5$. At $x=0.51$ an interpolated density of 5.67 g/cm$^{3}$ is calculated; we measure a density of 5.36 g/cm$^{3}$. The density deviates substantially from the linear interpolation for $0.6<x<0.95$, and for $x>0.95$ the density rises abruptly to rejoin the linearly interpolated value. 

An increase in density is expected to be associated with an increase in $\varepsilon_r$ \cite{Tomida2006, Kurtz2001}. The trends seen in Figure \ref{fig1} and Figure \ref{fig3} demonstrate that this correlation does not hold in either the Ta-Ge-O or Ta-Si-O system, though we note that for $x>0.95$ the dielectric constant does increase as the density increases. A detailed understanding of the structural evolution of these amorphous mixed oxides as a function of $x$ is required to further clarify the relation between local structure and dielectric constant. We speculate that \ce{GeO2} substitution initially disrupts the \ce{Ta2O5} local structure so that the density is reduced. Once a sufficient Ge concentration is reached, the distance between \ce{GeO4} tetrahedra becomes large and the density approaches a composition-averaged value. Deviation of the density with respect to linearly interpolated values does not correlate well with deviation of the dielectric constant as a function of composition.  

\subsection{Effect of polarizability on the dielectric constant}

Ion polarizabilities are often used to predict the dielectric constant of new materials \cite{Roberts,Devine2001}, although this approach is not well-founded theoretically. In practice the empirical polarizability of an ion is at best only approximately independent of its environment. The value of the approach is in identifying anomalous behavior. In particular, we note that there is no justification for assuming that the normal dependence $\varepsilon_{r} (x)$ in a mixed oxide system such as $\textnormal{M1}_{x}\textnormal{M2}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2}$ should be $\varepsilon_{r} (x) = x \cdot \varepsilon _{M1O_{2}} + (1-x) \cdot \varepsilon _{M2O_{2}}$. By comparison, in cases where there is no structural change it is plausible that the ion polarizability $\alpha _{i}$ of a given species $i$ might be constant, so that "normal" behavior for the overall polarizability of that system would be $\alpha _{total}(x) = x \cdot \alpha _{M1} + (1-x) \cdot \alpha _{M2} + 2\alpha _{O_{2}}$. The "normal" behavior for the dielectric constant would then be inferred using the Clausius-Mossotti (CM) relation
\begin{equation}
\frac{\varepsilon_r-1}{\varepsilon_r+2} = \frac{4\pi\alpha _{total}}{3 \varepsilon_0 V_{m}}
\label{eq2}
\end{equation} 
where the $\epsilon$ is the dielectric constant, $\alpha _{total}$ is the total dielectric polarizability, and $V_{m}$ is the molecular volume. This relation itself oversimplifies the role of depolarization by using a conventional depolarization factor ($4\pi / 3$) that is not appropriate for many crystalline materials although we suggest that it should be adequate for isotropic amorphous compounds. Using dielectric polarizabilities tabulated by Shannon \cite{Shannon1993a} ($\alpha(\ce{Ta}^{5+}) = 4.73\times10^{-24}$ cm$^{-3}$, $\alpha(\ce{O}^{2-}) = 2.01\times10^{-24}$ cm$^{-3}$, $\alpha(\ce{Si}^{4+}) = 0.87\times10^{-24}$ cm$^{-3}$, and $\alpha(\ce{Ge}^{4+}) = 1.63\times10^{-24}$ cm$^{-3}$) we can evaluate the overall polarizability. The value of $V_{m}$ can be inferred from the measured density of the amorphous films (Figure \ref{fig4}), so the dielectric constant can be calculated by rearranging Equation \ref{eq2} to give

The solid lines are linear interpolations between the values calculated for the endmembers.}
\label{fig4}
\end{figure}
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \frac{(3V_{m} + 8\pi\alpha_{total})}{(3V_{m}- 4\pi\alpha_{total})}
\label{eq3}
\end{equation}

The results are presented in Figure \ref{fig5}. For both Ta-Ge-O and Ta-Si-O the value of the calculated dielectric constants do not agree with experimental values. This implies that the static polarizability (i.e. corresponding to the low-frequency limit) associated with each ion is \emph{not} constant, as is the case in many potentially interesting material systems such as ferroelectrics \cite{Shannon1993a}. A more illuminating approach is to use the observed dielectric constant to infer the polarizability. We are most interested in distinguishing effects associated with low (phonon) frequencies versus those that operate only at high (optical) frequencies. First we infer the optical-frequency (electronic) polarizability, $\alpha _{e}$ using the Clausius-Mossotti equation in the form used for optical measurements (known as the Lorentz- Lorentz (LL) equation)

\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}+2} = \frac{4\pi\alpha_{e}}{3 V_{m}}.

The results are shown in Figure \ref{fig6}. As expected, the deviations from linear interpolation are not large. Some of the deviation is associated with the fact that the concentration of oxygen in the films is not constant; an attempt at quantitative accounting for that effect would overrate the significance of the concept of ion polarizability in this context. 
But the abrupt variation in $\alpha _{e}$ in the Ta-Ge-O system for the most Ta-rich compositions is noteworthy and suggests a substantial electronic reorganization. A realistic electronic structure calculation (e.g., using density functional theory) would be necessary to clarify the microscopic behavior.          

The ionic polarizability, $\alpha _{i}$, can be determined by subtracting the electronic term from the low frequency (static) polarizability $\alpha _{s}$ since $\alpha _{s} = \alpha _{e}+\alpha _{i}$. Figure \ref{fig6} illustrates the composition dependence of the ionic polarizability. It is immediately clear that a small increase in polarizability is obtained in the Ta-Si-O system; the largest percentage effect is seen for low Ta cation fractions, consistent with previous interpretations. This increase in $\alpha _{i}$ is offset by a slight decrease in $\alpha _{e}$ below x $<$ 0.75, resulting an essentially linear variation in $\varepsilon_r$. In the Ta-Ge-O system a very substantial increase in $\alpha_{i}$ is inferred for Ta-rich compositions. This strongly suggests that the dielectric enhancement is related to changes in vibrational structure rather than changes in density or modifications to the network. 

\subsection{FTIR}
Our analysis of the electronic and optical response of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ demonstrates the importance of $\alpha_{i}$, and therefore phonon modes, in the dielectric enhancement in Ta-Ge-O. We used FTIR to explore variation in the optically active phonon modes in the Ta-Ge-O system. Figure \ref{fig7} presents typical data (plotted as $1-R$) obtained using an instrument that can resolve reflectance down to 600 cm$^{-1}$. As expected, the spectra for the amorphous films exhibit only broad features due to disorder.

\begin{figure}
\noindent \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{chapter_4/fig7}
\centering
\caption{FTIR spectra for $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$. From bottom to top, x=0 (pure GeO$_{2}$), 0.35, 0.47, 0.64, 0.78, 0.85, 0.91, 0.94, 0.96, 0.97, 0.98, 1.0 (pure Ta$_{2}$O$_{5}$).}
\label{fig7}

The spectra for pure amorphous \ce{GeO2} (bottom most curve) shows two distinct features: a broad peak extending from 900 to 1000 cm$^{-1}$ and a peak $\simeq$850 cm$^{-1}$. The addition of \ce{GeO2} into \ce{Ta2O5} causes the peaks to shift to lower wavenumbers. 
The spectra eventually resembles that of pure amorphous \ce{Ta2O5} (top most curve), with a peak at $\simeq 630 $ cm$^{-1}$ and a broad feature that ranges from $750$cm $^{-1}$ to $850$ cm$^{-1}$. No anomalous features that indicate any modification of the network are observed. We speculate that the modes contributing directly to the dielectric enhancement are found at lower wavenumbers. 

\section{Conclusion}
Characterization of the structural and dielectric properties of Ta-Ge-O and Ta-Si-O amorphous oxide thin films shows that the low-frequency dielectric constant is greatly enhanced for Ta-Ge-O compositions x $>$ 0.75, while the refractive index shows essentially no enhancement. Direct measurement of the density revealed a slight correlation between density and the dielectric constant in Ta-Ge-O that is inadequate to draw a robust conclusion. The variation of the dielectric constant of Ta-Si-O with cation composition is essentially linear and only slight enhancements are observed. This trend is also reflected in the refractive index and density. 

A systematic relationship between atomic polarizabilities and the dielectric constant was found using the Clausius-Mossotti picture. In analyzing amorphous materials, the Clausius-Mossotti relationship has been one of few theoretical tools available that connect structure (density) and local response (polarizability) to an experimentally measured dielectric constant. 
In this study the densities were measured directly, thus we inferred the polarizabilities of the oxide without assuming constant individual ion polarizabilities. This enabled us to isolate the different polarizability mechanisms that are active at different frequencies. This analysis allows us to conclude that while the dielectric constant and density of Ta-Si-O are consistent with linear interpolation between endmember values, the polarizabilities are not. It is the simultaneous increase in ionic polarizability and suppression of electronic polarizability that makes the dielectric constant follow the linear interpolation. 
Not surprisingly, the ionic polarizability of Ta-Ge-O for high \ce{Ta2O5} compositions is enhanced, suggesting that \ce{GeO2} has a substantial effect on the vibrational structure of $\textnormal{Ta}_{x}\textnormal{Ge}_{1-x}\textnormal{O}_{2+x/2}$ even at very low \ce{GeO2} concentration. 

Finally, we explored the limitations of the Clausius-Mossotti picture in the context of amorphous Ta-Ge-O. We showed that calculating the dielectric constant using tabulated atomic polarizabilities and experimentally inferred molecular volumes systematically underestimates the measured dielectric constant. Only when the densities and dielectric properties are directly measured is one able to reveal underlying trends in the polarizabilities. Nevertheless, obtaining a complete and accurate picture of the local behavior of the amorphous oxide must involve sophisticated techniques such X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) in conjunction with computational simulations. These measurements are currently underway and are expected to shed light on the structure-property relations in complex amorphous oxides such as Ta-Ge-O.
